Keeping Your Seattle HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and schedule a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and is far less stressful than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of summer.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor costs little to replace, yet the same part, ignored through a full season, can ruin the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something sounds or feels wrong in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.

Common HVAC Problems in Seattle

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and salt air and moisture chew at outdoor condenser coils and fins, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly choke off airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much less expensive when caught early. If your system is doing anything unusual, new noises, weak airflow, rooms that won't hold temperature, bills creeping upward, a quick diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
